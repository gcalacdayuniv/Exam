## Complete Answer Key & Rationales

### Part 1: Multiple Choice Answers
1. **b** (Discounted Cash Flow) - *Rationale:* Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) estimates the absolute intrinsic value of an investment based strictly on discounting its expected future cash flows back to the present.
2. **c** (Adverse Selection) - *Rationale:* When the worst risks actively seek out insurance or bank financing before the contract is signed due to informational asymmetry, this is adverse selection.
3. **b** (Underwriting Spread) - *Rationale:* The underwriting spread represents the core profit margin earned by an investment house during a firm commitment underwriting scenario.
4. **b** (Best Efforts) - *Rationale:* In a "best efforts" agreement, the bank acts as an agent earning commissions, taking no inventory risk.
5. **d** (Duration) - *Rationale:* Duration is a specialized mathematical metric used to assess exactly how sensitive a bond's market price is to sudden fluctuations in interest rates.
6. **c** (The Float) - *Rationale:* The accumulated pool of unpaid premiums is called the "float," which insurers actively deploy into capital markets to generate investment income.
7. **a** (Arbitrage Pricing Theory) - *Rationale:* APT relies on predicting asset returns by measuring and correlating them with multiple macroeconomic variables.
8. **c** (Maintenance Margin) - *Rationale:* Dropping below the maintenance margin triggers a margin call, forcing the trader to immediately deposit cash to return the account to the initial margin level.
9. **b** (Dealer) - *Rationale:* A dealer acts as a principal, using its own capital to hold inventory (bonds) and profit from the bid-ask spread, unlike brokers who just act as agents.
10. **d** (Maturity Intermediation) - *Rationale:* Maturity intermediation is the core bank function of transforming highly liquid, short-term liabilities (deposits) into illiquid, long-term assets (commercial loans).
11. **c** (Risk-Based Capital) - *Rationale:* Capital surplus (RBC) is the mathematically calculated extra cushion required for unexpected market shocks, unlike everyday technical reserves.
12. **a** (Comparable Company Analysis) - *Rationale:* Comparable Company Analysis establishes a fair baseline acquisition price for a target business by rigorously evaluating the financial metrics of similar publicly traded peers.
13. **b** (Book Building Process) - *Rationale:* The book building process is used prior to an IPO to survey institutional investors, gauge demand volume, and determine the optimal clearing price for the new stock.
14. **c** (Money Market) - *Rationale:* The money market specifically handles short-term instruments with original maturities of exactly one year or less, primarily designated for managing short-term corporate liquidity.
15. **a** (Credit Risk) - *Rationale:* Default risk (Credit Risk) is the inherent danger regarding the timely failure of payment for scheduled interest and principal obligations.
16. **d** (Asset Liability Management) - *Rationale:* Asset Liability Management (ALM) dictates matching asset maturity timelines and risk profiles to the expected timelines of future liability payouts.
17. **c** (Conservatorship) - *Rationale:* When capital surplus drops below statutory requirements, the Insurance Commission steps in to place the company under Conservatorship to rehabilitate or orchestrate an orderly exit.
18. **b** (Law of One Price) - *Rationale:* The Law of One Price establishes the theoretical baseline that identical financial assets must eventually have identical prices across highly efficient markets.
19. **c** (Four Cs of Credit) - *Rationale:* Capacity, Collateral, Covenants, and Character (the Four Cs) are the traditional metrics used specifically to assess a borrower's overall credit risk.
