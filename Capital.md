## Complete Answer Key & Rationales

### Part 1: Multiple Choice Answers
1. **B** - *Rationale:* The BPO needs short-term liquidity (90 days). The money market specifically handles short-term instruments (≤ 1 year) like commercial paper. 
2. **A** - *Rationale:* Asset Management Companies focus on wealth preservation and deploying surplus funds to mitigate inflation without absorbing capital-raising risks, unlike investment houses.
3. **B** - *Rationale:* In a "best efforts" agreement, the bank acts as an agent earning commissions, taking no inventory risk. A firm commitment guarantees capital by buying the shares.
4. **B** - *Rationale:* Asset Liability Management (ALM) dictates matching asset maturities to liabilities. 30-year life policies require long-term, stable investments like sovereign bonds and real estate.
5. **B** - *Rationale:* Changing behavior to engage in higher risk *after* a contract is signed is the classic definition of post-contractual moral hazard.
6. **B** - *Rationale:* When the worst risks actively seek out insurance *before* the contract is signed due to informational asymmetry, this is adverse selection.
7. **A** - *Rationale:* The time value of money dictates that earlier cash flows have a higher present value. An annuity due pays at the beginning of the period, allowing the receiver to invest the money sooner.
8. **B** - *Rationale:* Inflation causes a general rise in the prices of goods, severely eroding the purchasing power (real value) of stagnant, uninvested capital over time.
9. **B** - *Rationale:* A credit union is a non-profit, member-owned depository institution that requires a shared bond and returns earnings to members rather than outside shareholders.
10. **B** - *Rationale:* There is an inverse relationship between interest rates and bond prices. When required market rates rise, the fixed rate of existing bonds becomes less attractive, driving their market price down.
11. **B** - *Rationale:* Arbitrage is the simultaneous purchase and sale of an asset in different markets to exploit pricing inefficiencies for a risk-free profit.
12. **B** - *Rationale:* When capital surplus drops below statutory requirements, the Insurance Commission steps in to place the company under Conservatorship to rehabilitate or orchestrate an orderly exit.
13. **A** - *Rationale:* Dropping below the maintenance margin triggers a margin call, forcing the trader to immediately deposit cash to return the account to the initial margin level.
14. **A** - *Rationale:* Maturity intermediation is the core bank function of transforming highly liquid, short-term liabilities (deposits) into illiquid, long-term assets (commercial loans).
15. **B** - *Rationale:* The national treasury is the largest deficit unit in an economy; its expenditures exceed receipts, requiring external capital from surplus units (households).
16. **B** - *Rationale:* A dealer acts as a principal, using its own capital to hold inventory (bonds) and profit from the bid-ask spread, unlike brokers who just act as agents.
17. **C** - *Rationale:* Capacity, Collateral, Covenants, and Character (the Four Cs) are the traditional metrics used specifically to assess Credit Risk (Default Risk).
18. **C** - *Rationale:* Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) estimates the absolute intrinsic value of an investment based strictly on discounting its expected future cash flows back to the present.
19. **B** - *Rationale:* Reserves are the mathematically calculated funds set aside to pay expected, everyday claims. Capital surplus (RBC) is the extra cushion for unexpected market shocks.
20. **D** - *Rationale:* The secondary market involves trading existing securities between investors. The issuing corporation receives no new capital from secondary market transactions.
21. **A** - *Rationale:* Underwriting syndication is utilized to distribute extreme financial risk among a group of different investment banks when a deal is too large for one institution.
22. **C** - *Rationale:* Insurers possess the "No Bank Run" advantage because their payouts are mathematically predictable and steep surrender penalties deter clients from mass panic withdrawals.
23. **B** - *Rationale:* The financial risk of mutual funds is borne entirely by the client. The AMC does not absorb the loss, but their revenue (based on Assets Under Management) drops predictably.
24. **B** - *Rationale:* Compounding is the process where previously earned interest is added to the principal to serve as the new, larger base for earning subsequent interest.
25. **C** - *Rationale:* The Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) is specifically purposed to calculate the expected/required return of an asset based on its systematic risk (beta).
26. **B** - *Rationale:* Over-the-Counter (OTC) markets are decentralized, bilateral dealer networks used for customized contracts, lacking the standardized rules of organized centralized exchanges.
27. **A** - *Rationale:* The book building process is used prior to an IPO to survey institutional investors, gauge demand volume, and determine the optimal clearing price for the new stock.
28. **B** - *Rationale:* The accumulated pool of unpaid premiums is called the "float," which insurers actively deploy into capital markets to generate investment income.
29. **A** - *Rationale:* A Transfer Agent is a specialized facilitator whose legal mandate is to countersign, monitor, and register stock to prevent unauthorized or fraudulent issuance.
30. **B** - *Rationale:* Mutual funds perform risk intermediation by pooling small amounts of retail capital to purchase diversified portfolios, a feat impossible for a small retail investor alone.
